THE RESEARCH

Abstract

Recent text-to-audio (TTA) models have advanced rapidly in generating diverse and realistic audio from text descriptions. Cross-attention plays an important role in incorporating textual information, yet our understanding of individual cross-attention heads remains limited. In this work, we define acoustic event realization (AER) as the generation of acoustic events described in the input caption and AER heads as cross-attention heads important for this process. We introduce event attention mass (EAM), a measure of the attention each head allocates to acoustic-event tokens. Across three TTA models, we rank cross-attention heads using EAM, identify the top-ranked heads as AER heads, and evaluate their influence through head masking. AER heads showed a stronger influence on acoustic event generation than random or bottom-ranked heads. We further show that AER heads more strongly influence text-to-audio generation performance, while low-ranked head masking largely preserved performance. These findings provide insight into the functional roles of individual cross-attention heads in TTA models and may support future research on acoustic-event control and the identification of other functional heads.

Index Terms: text-to-audio generation, acoustic event realization head, cross-attention, event attention mass

01 / AUDIO DEMONSTRATIONS

Impact of AER Head Masking on Acoustic Event Realization

To demonstrate the functional importance of AER heads, we compare audio generated under the baseline and three head-masking conditions—random, bottom-ranked, and AER head masking—using the same masking ratio. The target acoustic event is largely preserved under random or bottom-ranked head masking, but is suppressed or removed when AER heads are masked, supporting their functional importance in acoustic event realization.

02 / AUDIO DEMONSTRATIONS

Impact of Low-Ranked Head Masking

We progressively mask low-ranked heads based on the EAM ranking and compare the generated audio with the baseline. Audio quality and acoustic event realization are largely preserved over a substantial masking range, suggesting that low-ranked heads can be selectively masked with limited impact on generation.
